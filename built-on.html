01 / 03

Agents need context.

AI models get smarter every day. What they do not have is the context of your network. Without it, an agent is a brilliant new starter on its first day, every day.

The context layer turns your knowledge, expertise and processes into context a machine can use.

Knowledge

What exists. Sites, devices, circuits, and what depends on what.

Expertise

How the network is managed. What your engineers learned from outages and failed changes.

Processes

Which actions are acceptable, and who has to approve them.

Run like software: owned, approved, versioned and kept current.
Four steps to get there
01 / 04

Years of experience become playbooks.

Your best engineer knows which sites are critical and who to call, without looking anything up. That came from shadowing colleagues, living through outages and rolling back failed changes. The first step is to write it down.

02 / 03

Agents work through your tools.

An agent does not replace the systems a network team runs. It reads from them and writes to them, so tickets and audit trails stay where they are. The foundation is those connections, plus a platform for the agent to run on.

Service deskfor example  ServiceNowIncidents, change requests and problem records.
Monitoringfor example  SolarWinds OrionAlarms, and interface and device metrics.
Controllersfor example  Cisco DNA CenterDevice, client and wireless health. Approved actions run through here.
DDIfor example  InfobloxDNS, DHCP and address records.
CarriersCarrier portal or APICircuit status and carrier cases.
Agent platformInside your environmentWhere the agent runs, with its own access rights and a record of everything it does.
03 / 03

Autonomy is earned, then granted.

An agent with access to production needs tighter rules than a new engineer. It starts by observing and suggesting. You then decide, one task at a time, what it may do on its own.

The agent earns autonomy. You grant it. Every action has evidence, a named approver and a change record.
Collect diagnosticsLow riskOn its own
Clear a stuck portLow riskOn its own
Fail over a siteHigh riskWith approval

Read-only until you say otherwise

Every agent starts by observing and recommending. You choose which actions it may take.

A person approves every change

Nothing that alters production happens without a named engineer approving it.

Evidence with every answer

Each diagnosis shows the data it came from, so your team can check it in seconds.

Your tools stay in charge

Agents work through your service desk, monitoring and controllers. Records stay where they are.

Everything stays in your environment, and you own it.
