What is actually built, and what is a design

Three of these stages run against a live ServiceNow instance today: intake, estate matching, and the qualification tasks that stop the flow and ask a person a question. They are marked built and running today in the replay.

The rest are designed, written up and sequenced, but not built. I would rather show you the join than hide it, because the interesting part of this work is not the demo. It is which stages turned out to need a model at all.

Of the fourteen, one needs judgement from a model: reading a vendor's prose into comparable version ranges. The other thirteen are ordinary code. A version comparison that a model gets right 99 times in 100 is a version comparison that marks seven devices safe when they are not.

The numbers in the replay

755 devices, 10 built by another vendor, 12 inside the affected range, 7 of those at sites under regulatory validation, 0 reachable from the internet. Those came from a real match against a real instance, not from my imagination.

Where the honest answer was duller

I expected a perimeter emergency. Once the exposure data was repaired, none of the twelve faced the internet. It turned into a change-window and regulatory problem instead, which is less dramatic and much more like the real job.

What the agents are never allowed to do

Call a device qualified without a person confirming it. Send anything outside the organisation unapproved. Accept a risk. Submit a change that collides with another. Those are not settings, they are the shape of the flow.
